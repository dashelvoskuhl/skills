# Consumer Idea Lab

A repeatable method for finding consumer app ideas with the Stake, Polymarket and Kalshi feeling, without gambling or prediction markets.

## Output
- Lead with the answer. Short sentences. Phone-friendly blocks.
- Search before stating any fact about current companies, numbers, prices or laws, and cite it. End with a Sources list.

## Step 1: Generate wide
Write at least 15 raw ideas using these lenses:
- Institution-only, now one tap: something only museums, governments, billionaires or brands can do today.
- The internet vs. someone powerful.
- Collective control of a real thing: screens, robots, money, prices, buildings.
- Leverage: a tiny action with a huge visible effect ($1 that turns into $100 of impact).
- Real-world payoff: taps on a phone change something physical people can see.

## Step 2: Five filters (kill anything that fails one)
1. No way test: finish the sentence "No way I can ___ from my phone." A stranger should say "wait, what?"
2. Frequency: there must be a reason to open it daily. Look for one shared daily moment (Wordle's one puzzle a day), a comeback timer (r/place and Wplace pixel cooldowns) and a shareable result (Wordle's grid).
3. Funding: the crowd or sponsors pay for the spectacle, not the company. HQ Trivia paid prizes out of venture money and died.
4. Saturation: low to medium. Name the top 3 competitors and what each got wrong.
5. Buildability: a small team can launch a scrappy version in weeks. Flag gambling (prize plus chance plus paying to enter), securities, money transmission, age limits and physical safety. No gambling or prediction markets.

## Step 3: Dopamine stack (each finalist needs most of these)
- Instant hit: the second someone pays or taps, something with their name lights up.
- Proof card: an auto-made shareable card, the equivalent of the Wordle grid.
- Live moment: one daily appointment where the result is revealed, with a visible progress bar filling toward the goal.
- Crowd pitches: anyone proposes the next thing; it unlocks at a supporter threshold; the pitcher gets credit and a small cut.
- Cadence: small wins daily, a tentpole monthly.
- Losses still count: failed attempts become a collectible story, with refunds.
- Physical launch: a peel wall or real-world reveal that turns the launch into content.

## Step 4: Six-move playbook
For each finalist, fill in:
1. Rename it (what it is called so it feels new or legit)
2. Borrow trust (partners, press, institutions)
3. Legal wedge (the gap or rule that lets it launch)
4. Change who the user plays against (peer to peer, or the crowd vs. a billionaire)
5. Make using it into content (what people post)
6. Launch into a tentpole (the event to launch around)

## Step 5: Research each finalist (8 to 20 searches)
- Precedents with real numbers (users, dollars, time to scale)
- Current competitors, their funding and what went wrong
- Why now (new platform, law or cost drop)
- Money: who pays, how much, and what each night or unit costs to run
- Failure mode: exactly how similar companies died

## Step 6: Verdict
Rank the finalists. For each give: the one-line pitch, the "no way" sentence, the daily loop, how it makes money, the top 3 risks, a first cheap test (under $500 and 2 weeks) and a clear yes, maybe or no. Close with the bigger play if the ideas can share one brand.
