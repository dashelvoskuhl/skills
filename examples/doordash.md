# Example run: DoorDash, as if it were January 2013

Prompt: `Run the idea lab on this: delivery from the restaurants that don't deliver. Judge it with what a founder could know in January 2013.`

Run date: 2026-11-11. 13 web searches. The prompt supplied the idea, so the run starts at Step 2. The last two sections compare the verdict with what happened.

## Verdict: maybe

- **Pitch:** Delivery from the restaurants that have no drivers.
- **No way sentence:** "No way I can get delivery from a restaurant with no drivers from my phone."
- **Daily loop:** Hungry at dinner, order, eat, order again tomorrow.
- **Money:** A flat $6 delivery fee on each order [2][3].
- **Top 3 risks:** the $6 fee may not cover the driver, Grubhub can add drivers of its own, and the rules on contract drivers can change.
- **First cheap test:** One afternoon. A page with menus and a phone number, $6 a delivery, the founders drive. The real founders ran this exact test [2][4], and their first order came half an hour after launch [1].

The idea passes all five filters and the test costs an afternoon. It has one of the seven items in the dopamine stack, and the method asks for most of them. That mix earns a maybe.

## Step 2: Five filters

1. **No way test: weak pass.** Pizza delivery existed, so the idea shocks no one. The new part was every other restaurant, the ones with no drivers. A stranger phoned in an order from a bare page half an hour after it went up [1].
2. **Frequency: pass.** People eat every day and dinner is a shared daily moment. The idea has no comeback timer and no shareable result.
3. **Funding: pass.** The customer pays on each order. Kozmo.com delivered within an hour for no fee, raised about $250 million and shut down in April 2001 [5].
4. **Saturation: medium.**
   - Grubhub: an ordering platform for restaurants that employed their own delivery people [6]. A restaurant with no drivers had no way in.
   - Seamless: the same model. It merged with Grubhub in August 2013 [7].
   - Postmates: launched in 2011 [8]. The delivery companies of that period went after the major cities, which left the suburbs open [8].
5. **Buildability: pass, with flags.** Four students built the first version in an afternoon [2]. Flags: pay and status of contract drivers, tips, food safety, and listing restaurants that never agreed to it.

## Step 3: Dopamine stack

One of seven.

- **Cadence:** hit. Dinner comes every day.
- **Instant hit, proof card, live moment, crowd pitches, losses still count, physical launch:** miss. Food delivery is a habit, and it gives people nothing to post.

## Step 4: Six-move playbook

1. **Rename:** Palo Alto Delivery became DoorDash in June 2013 [1].
2. **Borrow trust:** Y Combinator backed the company in summer 2013 [3].
3. **Legal wedge:** none needed to start. The founders drove the first orders themselves [4].
4. **Opponent:** none. The idea has no game in it.
5. **Content:** none.
6. **Tentpole:** none.

## Step 5: Research

**Precedents**

- Grubhub and Seamless together listed about 28,800 restaurants at the end of 2013 [7]. Online ordering already worked at scale.

**Why now**

- 56% of American adults owned a smartphone by May 2013 [9].

**Failure mode**

- Kozmo.com: free delivery, no fee to cover the courier, gone in three years [5].

## What happened

- DoorDash passed Grubhub in US monthly sales in May 2019 [10].
- By 2024 it took 67% of US meal delivery sales, against 23% for Uber Eats [11].
- In the third quarter of 2026 it completed 993 million orders, handled $33.9 billion in marketplace order value and booked $4.6 billion in revenue [12].
- Grubhub sold for $650 million in 2024. Its previous buyer had agreed to pay $7.3 billion in 2020 [11].
- The flags were real. DoorDash paid $16.75 million in 2025 to settle a New York case over driver tips [13], and restaurants sued it over listings they had not approved [14].
- The path was rough. Sequoia's account of the company describes years of cash scarcity that came close to ending it [15].

## Where the method fell short

The method said maybe about a company that went on to take two thirds of its market. Its dopamine stack measures spectacle and sharing, and DoorDash grew on a daily need.

The saturation filter did find the opening. Its question about each competitor's mistake points at restaurants with no drivers and at the suburbs. Tony Xu credits the suburbs for the lead [14][16].
